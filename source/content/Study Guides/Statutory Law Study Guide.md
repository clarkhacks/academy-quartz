**Introductory Matters**  
**Chapter 1**

1. **Define the term "offense" and explain the different classifications.**
   - **Felony**: An offense for which a person may be sentenced to imprisonment for a term of which the maximum is more than one year
   - **Misdemeanor**: An offense so designated or for which the maximum term is one year or less
   - There are **five classes of felonies** and **four classes of misdemeanors**
   - **Class A Felony**: Most serious (e.g., first degree murder)
   - **Class E Felony**: Least serious felony
   - **Class A Misdemeanor**: Most serious misdemeanor (up to 1 year jail)
   - **Class D Misdemeanor**: Least serious misdemeanor

2. **Define the term "infraction".**
   - An **infraction** is so designated or, if no other sentence than a **fine or a fine and forfeiture or other civil penalty** is authorized
   - **The conviction of an infraction does not carry** with it the consequences of a **criminal conviction**
   - Examples: Traffic violations, minor municipal ordinance violations

3. **Describe when "prosecution is commenced": Misdemeanors, Felonies.**
   - **A prosecution is considered "commenced" when the information is filed** and/or **a felony complaint or indictment is filed**
   - For misdemeanors: When information or complaint is filed
   - For felonies: When felony complaint, information, or indictment is filed

4. **Define the term "included offense".**
   - **A person cannot be convicted of more than one offense** if one offense is included in the other, offenses are based on the same conduct or transaction, or the law does not state that each day's conduct counts as a separate offense
   - An included offense is a lesser offense that is necessarily committed when committing a greater offense
   - Example: Assault is included in robbery

5. **What are the requirements a police officer has when dealing with a deaf arrestee?**
   - Must provide reasonable accommodations for communication
   - May require interpreter services for Miranda warnings and interrogation
   - Written Miranda warnings may be provided
   - Officer should determine the person's preferred method of communication
   - Cannot proceed with interrogation without ensuring understanding

6. **Define the application process of the Criminal Activity Forfeiture Act.**
   - Allows seizure of property used in or derived from criminal activity
   - Requires probable cause that property was used in commission of a crime
   - Civil proceeding separate from criminal case
   - Property owner has right to contest forfeiture
   - Proceeds typically go to law enforcement agencies

7. **What offenses do the "24 Hour Rule" apply to?**
   - Typically applies to DWI/DUI cases
   - Suspect must be released within 24 hours unless formally charged
   - Applies to certain misdemeanor offenses where no warrant exists
   - Does not apply to felonies or cases with outstanding warrants

8. **What offenses are a recorded interview/interrogation required?**
   - First degree murder cases
   - Certain serious felonies as specified by jurisdiction
   - Cases involving juveniles (in many jurisdictions)
   - Sexual assault cases (recommended practice)
   - Any custodial interrogation for serious felonies

9. **Define the Time (Statute of) Limitations for prosecutions to commence.**
   - **Murder, rape in the first degree, attempted rape in the first degree, sodomy of any class** → **No limitation**
   - **A felony (not murder/rape)** → **3 years**
   - **Any misdemeanor** → **1 year**
   - **Any infraction** → **6 months**
   - **If accused found to lack mental fitness** → pause in limitation
   - **If DNA evidence later matches the accused** → prosecution allowed after identity established

10. **What are the 7 factors that can be motivated as a hate crime?**
    - Race
    - Color
    - Religion
    - National origin
    - Sex
    - Sexual orientation
    - Disability

**General Provisions**  
**Chapter 2**

1. **What are the two general requirements for criminal liability?**
   - **Actus Reus**: A voluntary act or omission (guilty act)
   - **Mens Rea**: A culpable mental state (guilty mind)

2. **Describe the different ways a voluntary act is part of the criminal liability requirements.**
   - **Voluntary bodily movement**: Conscious, willed action
   - **Omission**: Failure to act when there is a legal duty to act
   - **Possession**: Knowing control over contraband or evidence
   - Must be voluntary - reflexes, convulsions, or acts while unconscious don't qualify

3. **Identify and describe the four culpable mental states required for criminal liability.**
   - **Purpose**: Conscious object to cause the result or engage in conduct
   - **Knowledge**: Aware that conduct is of particular nature or circumstances exist
   - **Recklessness**: Consciously disregards substantial and unjustifiable risk
   - **Criminal Negligence**: Fails to be aware of substantial risk due to gross deviation from care expected
   - If not specified in statute: **Purpose**, **Knowledge**, or **Recklessness** is required
   - **Negligence is not sufficient** unless specifically stated

4. **When is a culpable mental state not required for certain offenses?**
   - For **infractions**
   - If the statute clearly says **no mental state required**
   - Strict liability offenses (rare in criminal law)

5. **Define the conditions for "Responsibility for the conduct of another."**
   - A person is responsible for their own conduct and:
   - **If they aid/plan/attempt with purpose**, they are criminally liable for the other person's conduct
   - Must have purpose to promote or facilitate the commission of the offense
   - Includes soliciting, encouraging, or assisting another person

6. **Define "entrapment" and give examples of how and when this can occur.**
   - **Requires 2 elements:**
     - Encouragement by law enforcement/agent
     - Defendant was **not already predisposed**
   - **Examples**: Undercover officer repeatedly pressuring reluctant person to buy drugs; creating criminal opportunity for someone not inclined to commit crime
   - **Not entrapment**: Providing opportunity to willing participant; using informants who don't induce crime

7. **How can a person's criminal liability be affected by being in an intoxicated or drugged condition?**
   - **Voluntary intoxication is not a defense**
   - **Involuntary intoxication is a defense** if it prevents knowing the criminal nature of the conduct
   - May negate specific intent for certain crimes
   - Does not excuse reckless or negligent conduct

**Justification – Use of Force**  
**Chapter 3**

1. **Define the basic requirements for the legal use of non-deadly force in self-defense or the defense of other persons.**
   - Reasonably believes it is necessary to protect themselves or another person
   - Actor is not the initial aggressor (or withdrew)
   - Force used must be proportional to threat
   - Imminent threat must exist

2. **Define the requirement(s) for the legal use of deadly force in either self-defense or the defense of other persons.**
   - Reasonably believes it is necessary
   - Person unlawfully enters/remains in a dwelling
   - Actor is not the initial aggressor (or withdrew)
   - Reasonably believes deadly force necessary to prevent death or serious physical injury

3. **Does a person have a duty to retreat before using force, (Deadly or Non Deadly)?**
   - **No duty to retreat:**
     - From dwelling, residence, or vehicle
     - From any place the person has the legal right to be
   - Missouri follows "Stand Your Ground" principle

4. **Define the requirements for the legal use of deadly and non-deadly force in the defense of premises.**
   - **Deadly force cannot be used just to protect property**
   - Allowed if person **reasonably believes** it is needed to prevent **property damage/tampering**
   - **Force must be terminated** as soon as the threat ends
   - Must reasonably believe force necessary to prevent unlawful entry

5. **Define the requirements for the legal use of deadly and non-deadly force in the defense of property.**
   - **Deadly force cannot be used just to protect property**
   - **Force must be terminated** when threat ends
   - Non-deadly force may be used to protect property from theft or damage
   - Must be reasonable and proportional

6. **Per the Missouri Criminal Code Handbook, define the term "Law Enforcement Officer."**
   - A person who by virtue of office or public employment is vested by law with a duty to maintain public order or to make arrests for offenses
   - Must be certified by POST (Peace Officer Standards and Training)
   - Includes police officers, sheriff's deputies, state troopers, federal agents with arrest authority

7. **Define the requirements for the legal use of deadly and non-deadly force when a law enforcement officer is making an arrest.**
   - **Force allowed if reasonably believes arrest is lawful**
   - **Deadly force only if:**
     - Immediate necessity
     - Felony involving serious injury
     - Attempting to escape using **deadly weapon** or **dangerous instrument**
     - Poses **serious danger** to officer or others

8. **State the Supreme Court ruling (Tennessee vs. Garner, 3/27/85) regarding the use of deadly force against a fleeing felon.**
   - Deadly force may not be used against unarmed, non-dangerous fleeing felon
   - Officer must have probable cause that suspect poses significant threat of death or serious physical injury
   - Overturned common law rule allowing deadly force against any fleeing felon
   - Must warn if feasible before using deadly force

9. **State a law enforcement officer's powers of arrest jurisdictionally, and when may an officer possess a firearm outside of their jurisdictional boundaries.**
   - **Officers can arrest with probable cause**
   - Must be **certified by POST** to gain arrest authority
   - **Officers can act based on reasonable appearances** (even if mistaken)
   - **Jurisdictional authority**: Generally within employing agency's jurisdiction
   - **Outside jurisdiction**: May act when in fresh pursuit, mutual aid agreements, or witnessing felony
   - **Firearm possession**: Generally allowed statewide for certified officers

10. **Define when a private person is justified in using deadly & non-deadly force in making an arrest.**
    - **Can use force** in 2 situations:
      - Making an arrest
      - Preventing escape if reasonably believes offense committed
    - Must reasonably believe person committed offense in their presence
    - Deadly force only if reasonably believes necessary to prevent escape and offense involved deadly force

**Criminal Statutes – Attempt and Conspiracy**  
**Chapter 4**

1. **Define the differences between the words "and" and "or" when used in the statutes.**
   - **"And"**: All elements must be present (conjunctive)
   - **"Or"**: Any one of the alternatives satisfies the requirement (disjunctive)
   - Critical for determining what prosecution must prove

2. **Define what an "element" is when examining statutes.**
   - Essential component that prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt
   - Each element must be established for conviction
   - Includes conduct, circumstances, and results specified in statute

3. **Define the elements of an "attempt" crime and a "conspiracy" crime.**
   - **Attempt Elements:**
     - Purpose to commit the offense
     - Performs an act that is a **substantial step** toward commission
   - **Conspiracy Elements:**
     - Person conspires to commit any offense
     - With 1+ persons
     - And 1+ does any act to further it

4. **Define the terms "substantial step" and "overt act."**
   - **Substantial step**: An act that is strongly corroborative of criminal purpose and goes beyond mere preparation (for attempt)
   - **Overt act**: Any act done to further the conspiracy, can be lawful act (for conspiracy)
   - Substantial step requires more than overt act

5. **Identify who are "special victims"**
   - Law enforcement, emergency personnel, parole/probation officers, jailers, highway/utility workers, elderly, disabled, vulnerable persons
   - All while in the **performance of official duties**
   - Enhanced penalties apply when these individuals are victims

**Homicide, Assaults, and Kidnapping**  
**Chapter 5**

1. **Identify the elements of and/or explain the differences between:**
   
   a. **First Degree Murder**
   - **Definition**: Knowingly causes the death of another **after deliberation**
   - **Deliberation**: *Cool reflection for any length of time, no matter how brief*
   - **Penalty**: Class A Felony – **Death** or **Life Imprisonment without parole/probation** (if over 18 at the time)
   
   b. **Murder in the Second Degree**
   - Knowingly causes death of another person
   - OR causes death while committing felony
   - No deliberation required
   - Class A Felony (10-30 years or life)
   
   c. **Voluntary Manslaughter**
   - Knowingly causes death of another in sudden passion arising from adequate cause
   - "Heat of passion" killing
   - Class B Felony (5-15 years)
   
   d. **Involuntary Manslaughter**
   - Recklessly causes death of another
   - OR causes death while committing misdemeanor
   - Class C Felony (3-10 years)
   
   e. **First, Second, Third, and Fourth Degree Assault of Special Victims**
   - **First Degree**: Attempts to kill or knowingly causes/attempts serious physical injury
   - **Second Degree**: Knowingly causes physical injury with deadly weapon or dangerous instrument
   - **Third Degree Assault**: Knowingly causes **physical injury** to another (Class E Felony, Class D if special victim)
   - **Fourth Degree Assault**: Attempts/recklessly causes injury, pain, or illness; places person in fear of immediate injury; offensive contact
   
   f. **Assault of an elderly person, a person with a disability or a vulnerable person**
   - Enhanced sentencing applies when victim is classified as "special victim"
   - Same elements as regular assault but higher penalties

2. **Identify the terms "aggravating" and "mitigating" as circumstances, and why they are considered in assessing the penalty for first degree murder.**
   - **Aggravating circumstances**: Factors that make crime more serious (multiple victims, torture, killing police officer, etc.)
   - **Mitigating circumstances**: Factors that reduce culpability (mental illness, duress, no prior record, etc.)
   - Used in death penalty cases to determine sentence
   - Jury weighs aggravating vs. mitigating factors

3. **Define the terms "deadly weapon" and "dangerous instrument"**
   - **Deadly Weapon** (*By design*): Firearm (loaded/unloaded), switchblade, dagger, billy club, blackjack, or metal knuckles
   - **Dangerous Instrument** (*By use*): Anything capable of causing death or serious injury in the manner it is used

4. **Identify the definition differences between "physical injury" and "serious physical injury."**
   - **Physical injury**: Impairment of physical condition or substantial pain
   - **Serious physical injury**: Physical injury that creates substantial risk of death or causes serious disfigurement or protracted loss/impairment of function

5. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **First Degree Assault** - Attempts to kill or knowingly causes/attempts serious physical injury with deadly weapon
   b. **Second Degree Assault** - Knowingly causes physical injury with deadly weapon or dangerous instrument
   c. **Third Degree Assault** - Knowingly causes **physical injury** to another
   d. **Fourth Degree Assault** - Attempts/recklessly causes injury, pain, or illness; places person in fear; offensive contact
   e. **Harassment** - Knowingly communicates threat to commit felony against person/property; repeatedly communicates anonymously; makes repeated unwanted communications

6. **Identify what is meant by the term "consent as a defense" when used in the offenses involving unlawful restraint.**
   - Victim's voluntary agreement to restraint
   - Must be informed and voluntary consent
   - Cannot consent to serious bodily harm in most cases
   - Consent obtained by fraud or duress is invalid

7. **Identify the major differences between the offenses of first degree kidnapping, second degree kidnapping, and third degree kidnapping.**
   - **First Degree**: Unlawful restraint with intent to hold for ransom, use as shield/hostage, facilitate felony/flight, inflict injury, terrorize victim
   - **Second Degree**: Unlawful restraint for substantial period under circumstances exposing victim to risk of serious physical injury
   - **Third Degree**: Unlawful restraint (basic kidnapping without aggravating factors)

**Sexual Assault Offenses**  
**Chapter 6**

1. **Define the terms "sexual intercourse", "deviate sexual intercourse", and "sexual contact".**
   - **Sexual intercourse**: Any penetration, however slight, of female sex organ by male sex organ
   - **Deviate sexual intercourse**: Sexual act involving genitals of one person and mouth, tongue, or anus of another
   - **Sexual contact**: Touching of genitals or anus for purpose of arousing/gratifying sexual desire

2. **Identify the specific ages and the term "forcible compulsion" that determines what specific sexual offense has been committed.**
   - **Under 12**: Statutory rape/sodomy 1st degree (regardless of consent)
   - **12-13**: Statutory rape/sodomy 1st degree if actor 21+ years old
   - **14-16**: Statutory rape/sodomy 2nd degree if actor 21+ years old
   - **Forcible compulsion**: Physical force, threat of death/injury/kidnapping that victim reasonably believes will be carried out

3. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **First-Degree Rape and First-Degree Sodomy**: Sexual intercourse/deviate sexual intercourse with person under 12, OR by forcible compulsion
   b. **Statutory Rape – First and Second Degrees**: 1st degree: under 12 or 12-13 with actor 21+; 2nd degree: 14-16 with actor 21+
   c. **Statutory Sodomy – First and Second Degrees**: Same age requirements as statutory rape but involving deviate sexual intercourse

4. **Additional Sexual Offenses:**
   d. **Second Degree Rape**: Sexual intercourse by forcible compulsion or with incapacitated person
   e. **Second Degree Sodomy**: Deviate sexual intercourse by forcible compulsion or with incapacitated person
   f. **Sexual Misconduct – First and Second Degrees**: 1st: sexual intercourse with person under 14; 2nd: deviate sexual intercourse with person under 15
   g. **Sexual Misconduct Involving a Child**: Sexual contact with child under 15
   h. **Sexual Abuse – First and Second Degrees**: 1st: sexual contact by forcible compulsion; 2nd: sexual contact with incapacitated person
   i. **Child Molestation – First and Second Degrees**: 1st: sexual contact with child under 12; 2nd: sexual contact with child under 12 by person under 17

5. **Identify the elements of the offense Reckless Exposure to HIV.**
   - Person infected with HIV
   - Recklessly infects or exposes another through sexual intercourse or sharing needles
   - Without informing the other person of HIV status
   - Class B Felony

6. **Exposing Another Person to a Serious Infectious or Communicable Disease**
   - Knowingly exposing another to serious infectious disease
   - Through sexual contact, sharing needles, or other transmission methods
   - Without disclosure of condition

**Drug Offenses**  
**Chapter 7**

1. **Identify where and how "controlled substances" are defined.**
   - Defined in Chapter 195 RSMo (Missouri Controlled Substances Act)
   - Classified in Schedules I-V based on medical use and abuse potential
   - Schedule I: High abuse potential, no accepted medical use (heroin, LSD)
   - Schedule V: Low abuse potential, accepted medical use (cough medicines with codeine)

2. **Identify or define the terms "actual possession" and "constructive possession" as they relate to Chapter 195 RSMo.**
   - **Actual possession**: Physical possession on person or within immediate reach
   - **Constructive possession**: Control over substance even if not physically present (in car, home, etc.)
   - Must have knowledge and ability to control

3. **Define and give examples of "sole and joint possession".**
   - **Sole possession**: Only one person has control over substance
   - **Joint possession**: Multiple people share control (roommates with drugs in shared space)
   - All parties with joint possession can be charged

4. **Identify the elements of "possession" of a controlled substance**
   - Knowledge of presence of controlled substance
   - Knowledge of its nature as controlled substance
   - Control over the substance (actual or constructive)

5. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Delivery of a Controlled Substance**: Transfer or attempt to transfer controlled substance to another person
   b. **Manufacture of a Controlled Substance**: Production, preparation, compounding, or processing of controlled substance

6. **Identify the elements of "use or possession of drug paraphernalia."**
   - Possession of equipment used for drug consumption
   - Knowledge that items are drug paraphernalia
   - Intent to use for controlled substance activity

7. **Identify the elements of "possession of imitation controlled substances."**
   - Possession of substance represented as controlled substance
   - Intent to distribute as controlled substance
   - Substance is not actually controlled substance

8. **Identify the only situation where possession of a controlled substance is a misdemeanor.**
   - Possession of 35 grams or less of marijuana or synthetic cannabinoid
   - First or second offense
   - Class A Misdemeanor

9. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Distribution of a Controlled Substance near Schools**: Distribution within 2000 feet of school property; enhanced penalties apply
   b. **Distribution near Public Housing**: Distribution within 1000 feet of public housing; enhanced penalties apply

**Robbery, Arson, Burglary and Related Offenses**  
**Chapter 8**

1. **Identify the elements of:**
   A. **Robbery in the Second Degree**
   - **Elements**: Forcibly steals and causes physical injury to another person
   
   B. **Robbery in the First Degree**
   - **Elements**: Forcibly steals and:
     1. Causes serious injury, or
     2. Uses a deadly weapon, or
     3. Uses/threatens dangerous instrument, or
     4. Threatens with deadly weapon appearance, or
     5. Steals a controlled substance from a pharmacy

2. **Define the term "inhabitable structure"**
   - Any structure, vehicle, or watercraft where person lives or carries on business
   - Includes houses, apartments, mobile homes, businesses
   - Must be used for lodging or business purposes

3. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Arson**
   - **Third degree**: Knowingly damages building by starting fire/explosion
   - **Second degree**: Knowingly damages inhabitable structure by fire/explosion
   - **First degree**: Knowingly damages inhabitable structure by fire/explosion and person present or firefighter/law enforcement injured
   
   b. **Knowingly Burning or Exploding**: Intentionally starting fire/explosion
   c. **Reckless Burning or Exploding**: Recklessly starting fire/explosion that damages property
   d. **Negligent Burning & Exploding**: Negligently starting fire/explosion
   e. **Causing a Catastrophe**: Knowingly causing widespread injury/damage by explosion, fire, flood, etc.

4. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Burglary in the Second Degree**: Knowingly enters unlawfully or remains unlawfully in building/inhabitable structure
   b. **Burglary in the First Degree**: Burglary 2nd degree PLUS: building/structure is dwelling, person present, or actor armed
   c. **Business open to public**: Can still be burglary if person enters area not open to public or remains after closing
   d. **Aggravating circumstances for 1st degree**: Dwelling, person present, or armed with deadly weapon
   e. **Possession of Burglar's Tools**: Difficult to prove intent; tools must be specifically designed for burglary

5. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Tampering in the First Degree**: Tampering with property and creates substantial risk to person/property
   b. **Tampering in the Second Degree**: Tampering with property of another without consent

6. **State how the tampering statutes apply to operators and passengers in stolen vehicles.**
   - Operators: Can be charged with tampering if they knowingly operate stolen vehicle
   - Passengers: Can be charged if they know vehicle is stolen and remain in vehicle

7. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Property Damage in the First Degree**: Knowingly damages property of another >$750
   b. **Property Damage in the Second Degree**: Knowingly damages property of another ≤$750
   c. **Trespass in the First Degree**: Knowingly enters unlawfully or remains unlawfully on property after notice
   d. **Trespass in the Second Degree**: Enters or remains unlawfully on property

**Stealing and Related Offenses**  
**Chapter 9**

1. **Identify the elements of stealing.**
   - **Elements**: Appropriates property or services without consent and with intent to deprive (via deceit/coercion)

2. **Identify what value determines when stealing becomes a felony.**
   - **Felony Threshold**: $750.00 is the felony value threshold for most property
   - **Exception**: Threshold remains **$50.00** for financial exploitation of elderly/disabled

3. **Identify how to determine the dollar value used in stealing offenses.**
   - Value determines felony/misdemeanor
   - For merchants, value is the regular replacement cost
   - Fair market value at time of theft
   - Replacement cost for unique items

4. **Other than the value of something, identify what other elements will make stealing a felony offense.**
   - Prior guilty findings of 3 stealing-related offenses within 10 years
   - Theft of a catalytic converter
   - Theft of mail (letter, postal card, package, etc.) collected for delivery
   - Theft of: Motor vehicle/aircraft, Credit/debit device, Firearm, Livestock, Controlled substance

5. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Passing Bad Checks**
   - Makes/passes check knowing it won't be honored
   - Presents check with no account or insufficient funds
   - No payment within 10 days of notice
   
   b. **Fraudulent Use of a Credit Device**
   - Fraudulent use of a credit/debit device = **class A misdemeanor**
   - If value ≥ $750 or 3+ offenses in 30 days = **class E felony**

6. **Identify what is meant by the provision "physically taken from the person of the victim."**
   - Property must be taken directly from victim's person or immediate control
   - Enhances theft to robbery when combined with force
   - Includes items in victim's hands, pockets, or immediate reach

7. **Identify why "claim of right" applies to stealing offenses.**
   - Good faith belief that one has right to property
   - Negates intent to steal
   - Must be honest belief, even if mistaken
   - Does not apply to clearly illegal taking

8. **Identify what is meant by "purpose to defraud", and the significance of a ten-day letter.**
   - **Purpose to defraud**: Intent to deceive and cause financial loss
   - **Ten-day letter**: Notice to check writer demanding payment within 10 days
   - Failure to pay within 10 days creates presumption of intent to defraud

9. **Define a credit and debit device.**
   - **Credit device**: Includes writing, code, or number authorizing payment
   - **Debit device**: Includes cards used to electronically transfer funds

10. **Identify the elements of Identity Theft, Trafficking in Stolen Identities and Forgery.**
    - **Identity Theft**: Knowingly uses another's personal identifying information without consent to obtain benefit
    - **Forgery**: Intent to defraud, and make, complete, alter, or authenticate any writing to appear as though made by another person
    - **Trafficking in Stolen Identities**: Selling/manufacturing 5+ means of identification of separate persons with intent to commit fraud

**Armed Criminal Action and Weapons Offenses**  
**Chapter 10**

1. **Define the following terms:**
   a. **Concealable Firearms**: Firearms designed to be carried and concealed on person
   b. **Concealed Firearm**: Firearm carried hidden from ordinary observation
   c. **Dangerous Instrument**: Anything capable of causing death or serious injury in the manner it is used
   d. **Deadly Weapon**: Firearm (loaded/unloaded), switchblade, dagger, billy club, blackjack, or metal knuckles
   e. **Knife**: Dagger, dirk, stiletto, or similar; not pocketknives with blades < 4 inches

2. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Armed Criminal Action**
   - Committing a felony, AND
   - Doing so with/through the use of a deadly weapon or dangerous instrument
   
   b. **Possession, Manufacture, Transport, Repair, or Sale of Certain Weapons Prohibited**
   - Knowingly manufacture, sell, repair, transport: explosive weapons, projectiles with explosive/chemical charge, gas guns, switchblade knives, knuckles, any federally prohibited weapon
   
   c. **Unlawful Use of Weapons**: Exhibiting weapon in angry/threatening manner; carrying firearm/explosive weapon while intoxicated; discharging firearm within city limits
   
   d. **Defacing Firearms**
   - **Class A Misdemeanor**
   - Knowingly defacing any firearm (removal of serial number)
   
   e. **Possession of a Defaced Firearm**
   - **Class B Misdemeanor**
   - Knowingly possessing a firearm that is defaced
   
   f. **Unlawful Transfer of Weapons**: Selling/transferring firearm to prohibited person; straw purchases

**Offenses Against Public Order**  
**Chapter 11**

1. **Identify the elements of Peace Disturbance.**
   a. **Peace Disturbance**: Unreasonably and knowingly disturbs or alarms another person by loud noise, offensive language, threatening, fighting, creating hazardous condition
   b. **Private Peace Disturbance**: Same conduct but on private property

2. **Describe the major differences between the above two (2) statutes.**
   - **Peace Disturbance**: Occurs in public place or affects public
   - **Private Peace Disturbance**: Occurs on private property but still affects others
   - Both require unreasonable conduct that disturbs others

3. **Define the terms:**
   a. **Property of Another**: Property in which person does not have possessory interest
   b. **Private Property**: Property owned by individual or private entity
   c. **Public Place**: Property owned by government or open to public

4. **State the minimum number of people that must "assemble" for the unlawful assembly and rioting statutes to apply.**
   - **Six or more persons** must assemble for unlawful assembly
   - Same number applies to rioting statutes

5. **For the unlawful assembly and rioting statutes to apply, state what "agreement" must be made between the participating parties.**
   - Agreement to violate criminal law
   - Agreement to commit acts of violence
   - Common purpose to engage in unlawful conduct

6. **Identify the elements of Invasion of Privacy.**
   - Knowingly photographs, films, or records another person without consent
   - In state of full or partial nudity
   - In place where person has reasonable expectation of privacy

**Offenses Against Justice**  
**Chapter 12**

1. **Identify the elements of:**
   a. **Hindering Prosecution**: Harbors/conceals person; provides false information; prevents discovery of crime
   b. **False Reports**: Knowingly gives false information to law enforcement about crime
   c. **Resisting or interfering with Arrest**: Preventing/attempting to prevent arrest of self or another
   d. **Refusal to Identify as a Witness**: Refusing to provide name when lawfully detained as witness
   e. **Escape or Attempted Escape from Custody**: Departing from custody without lawful authority
   f. **Tampering With a Witness**: Inducing witness to testify falsely or withhold testimony
   g. **Tampering With a Victim**: Inducing victim to withhold testimony or give false testimony
   h. **Bribery of a Public Servant**: Offering benefit to public servant to influence official action
   i. **Perjury**: Making false statement under oath in official proceeding
   j. **Public Servant Acceding to Corruption**: Public servant accepting benefit for official action
   k. **Misuse of Official Information**: Using confidential information for personal gain

2. **State what makes the offenses of concealing an offense, hindering prosecution, and resisting or interfering with arrest, a felony or a misdemeanor.**
   - **Felony** if the underlying offense being concealed/hindered is a felony
   - **Misdemeanor** if the underlying offense is a misdemeanor
   - **Resisting arrest** is typically a misdemeanor unless it involves violence or weapons

3. **Identify the major differences between Resisting or Interfering with Arrest, and between Escape from Custody and Attempted Escape from Custody.**
   - **Resisting/Interfering with Arrest**: Preventing arrest before it's completed; person not yet in custody
   - **Escape from Custody**: Person already in lawful custody and departs without authority
   - **Attempted Escape**: Trying to escape but unsuccessful; still requires substantial step toward escape

## **KEY DIFFERENCES SECTION**
*Critical distinctions between similar offenses and legal concepts*

### **HOMICIDE OFFENSES**

**First Degree Murder vs. Second Degree Murder**
- **First Degree**: Requires **deliberation** (cool reflection, however brief) + knowingly causes death
- **Second Degree**: **No deliberation required** - just knowingly causes death OR causes death during felony
- **Penalty**: Both Class A Felony, but 1st degree can result in death penalty

**Murder vs. Manslaughter**
- **Murder**: **Knowingly** causes death (with or without deliberation)
- **Voluntary Manslaughter**: Knowingly causes death in **sudden passion** from adequate cause
- **Involuntary Manslaughter**: **Recklessly** causes death OR causes death during misdemeanor

**Voluntary vs. Involuntary Manslaughter**
- **Voluntary**: **Knowingly** kills in heat of passion (Class B Felony)
- **Involuntary**: **Recklessly** kills or kills during misdemeanor (Class C Felony)

### **ASSAULT OFFENSES**

**Assault Degrees (1st through 4th)**
- **First Degree**: Attempts to **kill** OR knowingly causes/attempts **serious physical injury** with deadly weapon
- **Second Degree**: Knowingly causes **physical injury** with deadly weapon/dangerous instrument
- **Third Degree**: Knowingly causes **physical injury** (no weapon required)
- **Fourth Degree**: **Attempts/recklessly** causes injury OR places in **fear** OR **offensive contact**

**Physical Injury vs. Serious Physical Injury**
- **Physical Injury**: Impairment of condition or substantial pain
- **Serious Physical Injury**: Creates substantial risk of **death** OR causes **disfigurement** OR **protracted loss of function**

### **SEXUAL OFFENSES**

**Rape vs. Sodomy**
- **Rape**: **Sexual intercourse** (penetration of female sex organ by male sex organ)
- **Sodomy**: **Deviate sexual intercourse** (mouth/tongue/anus contact with genitals)

**First Degree vs. Second Degree (Rape/Sodomy)**
- **First Degree**: Victim **under 12** OR by **forcible compulsion**
- **Second Degree**: By **forcible compulsion** OR with **incapacitated** person (but victim over 12)

**Statutory Rape/Sodomy Degrees**
- **First Degree**: Victim **under 12** OR victim **12-13** with actor **21+ years old**
- **Second Degree**: Victim **14-16** with actor **21+ years old**

### **THEFT OFFENSES**

**Stealing vs. Robbery**
- **Stealing**: Taking property **without consent** with intent to deprive
- **Robbery**: **Forcibly steals** (uses/threatens force) + causes injury or uses weapon

**Robbery Degrees**
- **First Degree**: Forcibly steals + (**serious injury** OR **deadly weapon** OR **dangerous instrument**)
- **Second Degree**: Forcibly steals + **physical injury**

**Actual vs. Constructive Possession**
- **Actual**: Physical possession on person or immediate reach
- **Constructive**: Control over item even when not physically present (in car, home, etc.)

### **BURGLARY OFFENSES**

**Burglary Degrees**
- **First Degree**: Unlawful entry/remaining + (**dwelling** OR **person present** OR **armed**)
- **Second Degree**: Basic unlawful entry/remaining in building/inhabitable structure

**Burglary vs. Trespass**
- **Burglary**: Unlawful entry with **intent to commit crime** inside
- **Trespass**: Simply entering/remaining unlawfully (no additional criminal intent required)

### **WEAPONS OFFENSES**

**Deadly Weapon vs. Dangerous Instrument**
- **Deadly Weapon**: Dangerous **by design** (firearm, switchblade, dagger, etc.)
- **Dangerous Instrument**: Dangerous **by use** (anything capable of causing death/serious injury in manner used)

**Armed Criminal Action vs. Unlawful Use of Weapons**
- **Armed Criminal Action**: Using deadly weapon/dangerous instrument **while committing felony**
- **Unlawful Use**: Exhibiting weapon threateningly, carrying while intoxicated, etc. (standalone offense)

### **DRUG OFFENSES**

**Possession vs. Delivery vs. Manufacturing**
- **Possession**: Having control over controlled substance (actual or constructive)
- **Delivery**: **Transfer** or attempt to transfer to another person
- **Manufacturing**: **Production, preparation, compounding** of controlled substance

**Sole vs. Joint Possession**
- **Sole**: Only one person has control
- **Joint**: Multiple people share control (all can be charged)

### **MENTAL STATES**

**Four Culpable Mental States (Highest to Lowest)**
1. **Purpose**: Conscious objective to cause result
2. **Knowledge**: Aware conduct is of particular nature
3. **Recklessness**: Consciously disregards substantial risk
4. **Criminal Negligence**: Fails to be aware of substantial risk due to gross deviation

**Voluntary vs. Involuntary Intoxication**
- **Voluntary**: **Not a defense** to criminal liability
- **Involuntary**: **Is a defense** if prevents knowing criminal nature of conduct

### **USE OF FORCE**

**Deadly vs. Non-Deadly Force**
- **Deadly Force**: Force likely to cause death or serious physical injury
- **Non-Deadly Force**: Force not likely to cause death or serious physical injury

**Self-Defense vs. Defense of Property**
- **Self-Defense**: Can use **deadly force** to prevent death/serious injury
- **Defense of Property**: **Cannot use deadly force** just to protect property

**LEO vs. Private Person Use of Force**
- **LEO**: Can use deadly force against **fleeing dangerous felon**
- **Private Person**: Very limited - only if offense involved deadly force and necessary to prevent escape

### **ATTEMPT VS. CONSPIRACY**

**Attempt vs. Conspiracy**
- **Attempt**: **Individual** crime - person tries to commit offense with **substantial step**
- **Conspiracy**: **Group** crime - agreement between **2+ people** with **overt act**

**Substantial Step vs. Overt Act**
- **Substantial Step**: Must **strongly indicate criminal intent** and go beyond preparation
- **Overt Act**: **Any act** (even lawful) done to further conspiracy

### **ARREST AND DETENTION**

**Arrest vs. Terry Stop**
- **Arrest**: Taking into custody - requires **probable cause**
- **Terry Stop**: Brief detention - requires **reasonable suspicion**

**Probable Cause vs. Reasonable Suspicion**
- **Probable Cause**: **More likely than not** that crime occurred
- **Reasonable Suspicion**: **Specific, articulable facts** suggesting criminal activity

### **SPECIAL VICTIMS**

**Special Victim vs. Regular Victim**
- **Special Victim**: Enhanced penalties apply (LEO, elderly, disabled, etc.)
- **Regular Victim**: Standard penalties apply

**On Duty vs. Off Duty (Special Victims)**
- **On Duty**: Special victim status applies for LEO, emergency personnel
- **Off Duty**: May not qualify for enhanced penalties (depends on circumstances)

### **COURT PROCEEDINGS**

**Felony vs. Misdemeanor vs. Infraction**
- **Felony**: **More than 1 year** imprisonment possible
- **Misdemeanor**: **1 year or less** imprisonment possible  
- **Infraction**: **Fine only** - no criminal conviction consequences

**Information vs. Indictment vs. Complaint**
- **Information**: Formal charge filed by **prosecutor**
- **Indictment**: Formal charge issued by **grand jury**
- **Complaint**: **Initial** charging document

## **LEGAL DEFINITIONS GLOSSARY**
*Key terms and complicated words used throughout this study guide*

### **A**
- **Actus Reus**: The physical act or conduct that constitutes a criminal offense; the "guilty act"
- **Actor**: The person whose criminal liability is in question
- **Adequate Cause**: Sufficient provocation that would cause a reasonable person to lose self-control
- **Affirmative Defense**: A defense that admits the basic facts but provides justification or excuse
- **Aggravating Circumstances**: Factors that increase the severity or culpability of a crime
- **Another**: Any person other than the actor
- **Appropriate**: To take, obtain, use, transfer, conceal, or retain property without consent
- **Armed Criminal Action**: Commission of a felony through use, assistance, or aid of a deadly weapon or dangerous instrument
- **Arrest**: Taking a person into custody in a case and in the manner authorized by law

### **B**
- **Benefit**: Gain or advantage, or anything regarded by the beneficiary as gain or advantage
- **Beyond Reasonable Doubt**: The standard of proof required in criminal cases; highest burden of proof
- **Bodily Injury**: Physical pain, illness, or any impairment of physical condition
- **Building**: Any structure or edifice enclosing a space or area, with walls and a roof
- **Burden of Proof**: The obligation to prove allegations by presenting evidence

### **C**
- **Coercion**: Threat of unlawful force upon a person or another
- **Complaint**: A charge, preferred before a magistrate having jurisdiction, that a person has committed a specified offense
- **Conduct**: An act or series of acts, and the accompanying mental state
- **Conjunctive**: Connected by "and" - all elements must be present
- **Consent**: Assent in fact, whether express or apparent
- **Constructive Possession**: Legal control over property even when not physically present
- **Controlled Substance**: A drug, substance, or immediate precursor included in Schedules I through V
- **Corroborative**: Evidence that supports or confirms other evidence
- **Credit Device**: A writing, number, code, or other evidence of an account with a creditor
- **Criminal Negligence**: The mental state of a person who fails to be aware of a substantial and unjustifiable risk
- **Culpable**: Deserving blame or censure; at fault
- **Custodial Interrogation**: Questioning by law enforcement while person is in custody
- **Custody**: Restraint of a person pursuant to arrest or the order of a court

### **D**
- **Deliberation**: Cool reflection, however brief, before acting
- **Deviate Sexual Intercourse**: Sexual contact involving mouth, tongue, or anus with genitals
- **Disfigurement**: Permanent damage to appearance or form
- **Disjunctive**: Connected by "or" - any one alternative satisfies the requirement
- **Duress**: Unlawful pressure or constraint forcing someone to act against their will

### **E**
- **Element**: Essential component of a crime that prosecution must prove
- **Entrapment**: Government inducement of a crime by someone not predisposed to commit it
- **Exigent Circumstances**: Emergency situations requiring immediate action

### **F**
- **Felony**: Crime punishable by more than one year imprisonment
- **Forcible Compulsion**: Physical force or threat that victim reasonably believes will be carried out
- **Fresh Pursuit**: Immediate and continuous pursuit of a fleeing suspect

### **G**
- **Gross Deviation**: Extreme departure from standard of care expected

### **I**
- **Imminent**: About to happen immediately; impending
- **Incapacitated**: Unable to consent due to mental or physical condition
- **Included Offense**: Lesser crime necessarily committed when committing greater offense
- **Infraction**: Minor violation punishable only by fine, not considered criminal conviction
- **Inhabitable Structure**: Building, vehicle, or watercraft used for lodging or business

### **J**
- **Jurisdictional**: Relating to the authority to enforce laws in a specific area

### **M**
- **Mens Rea**: The mental state or intent required for criminal liability; "guilty mind"
- **Misdemeanor**: Crime punishable by one year or less imprisonment
- **Mitigating Circumstances**: Factors that reduce the severity or culpability of a crime
- **Mutual Aid Agreement**: Formal arrangement between law enforcement agencies to assist each other

### **O**
- **Overt Act**: Any act, lawful or unlawful, done to further a conspiracy

### **P**
- **Paraphernalia**: Equipment or apparatus used for illegal drug activity
- **POST**: Peace Officer Standards and Training - certification body for law enforcement
- **Predisposed**: Already inclined or willing to commit a crime
- **Probable Cause**: Reasonable belief that a crime has been committed
- **Protracted**: Extended over a long period of time
- **Purpose**: Conscious objective to cause a particular result

### **R**
- **Reasonable Belief**: What a reasonable person would believe under similar circumstances
- **Recklessness**: Consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk

### **S**
- **Statutory**: Created by or relating to written laws (statutes)
- **Straw Purchase**: Buying something on behalf of someone who cannot legally purchase it
- **Substantial Step**: Act that strongly indicates criminal intent and goes beyond preparation
- **Substantial Risk**: Significant probability of harm or danger

### **T**
- **Terry Stop**: Brief investigative detention based on reasonable suspicion

### **U**
- **Unlawful**: Against the law; illegal
- **Unjustifiable Risk**: Risk that cannot be reasonably defended or excused

### **V**
- **Voluntary Act**: Conscious, willed bodily movement
- **Vulnerable Person**: Individual who lacks capacity to consent or protect themselves

### **Legal Phrases and Latin Terms**
- **In Camera**: In private; in the judge's chambers
- **Per Se**: By itself; inherently
- **Prima Facie**: On its face; at first appearance
- **Res Gestae**: Things done; part of the action
- **Sine Qua Non**: Essential element; without which nothing

### **Criminal Law Concepts**
- **Attempt**: Trying to commit a crime with substantial step toward completion
- **Conspiracy**: Agreement between two or more people to commit a crime
- **Solicitation**: Asking, encouraging, or commanding someone to commit a crime
- **Accomplice**: Person who helps another commit a crime
- **Principal**: Person who directly commits the crime
- **Accessory**: Person who helps before or after the crime but not during

### **Evidence Terms**
- **Circumstantial Evidence**: Indirect evidence that implies a fact
- **Direct Evidence**: Evidence that directly proves a fact
- **Hearsay**: Out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter
- **Chain of Custody**: Documentation of evidence handling from collection to court

### **Procedural Terms**
- **Information**: Formal criminal charge filed by prosecutor
- **Indictment**: Formal charge issued by grand jury
- **Complaint**: Initial charging document
- **Arraignment**: Court appearance where charges are read and plea entered
- **Preliminary Hearing**: Court proceeding to determine if probable cause exists
