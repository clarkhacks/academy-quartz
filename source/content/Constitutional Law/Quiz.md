### PART IV: SHORT ANSWER SAMPLE ANSWERS

  

81. **Present Memory Refreshed vs. Past Recollection Recorded:**

Present memory refreshed occurs when an officer uses a report to refresh their memory but then testifies from memory. The report itself is not evidence. Past recollection recorded is used when the officer cannot remember even with the report. The officer must verify the report was made when memory was fresh, and the writing itself becomes the evidence.

  

82. **Dying Declaration Requirements:**

Traditional requirements: (1) Declarant must believe death is imminent, (2) Statement concerns cause/circumstances of impending death, (3) Declarant must actually die, (4) Only admissible in homicide prosecutions. Modern application: If declarant survives but is permanently unable to testify (coma, brain damage), the declaration may still be admissible under other hearsay exceptions like excited utterance.

  

83. **Unidentified Informant Testimony:**

Four factors: (1) Proven track record of reliability, (2) Testifies against self/others, (3) Provided detailed, compelling information, (4) Information corroborated by officer. These factors establish reliability despite protected identity.

  

84. **Business Records Exception:**

Business records are admissible because they are assumed accurate due to regular business practices. Conditions: (1) Made in regular course of business, (2) No reason to suspect the recordkeeping process is in question, (3) Person who made them is absent. Examples include sales receipts, contracts, bills of lading.

  

85. **Public Records Exception:**

Public records are admissible because government officials have no motive to falsify routine documents. They include minutes, tax records, license applications. Exception: Lab technician reports do NOT qualify as public records because the analysis process itself may be questioned.

  

86. **Fruit of the Poisonous Tree:**

This doctrine excludes evidence derived from unlawful police conduct. The primary illegally obtained evidence is the "poisonous tree," and any evidence derived from it is the "fruit." Example: Illegal search finds key, key used to search home - both key and evidence from home are excluded unless exception applies.

  

87. **Good Faith Exception:**

Applies when officer reasonably relies on defective warrant issued by judge, dispatcher, or clerk. Does NOT apply when: (1) Officer falsifies information, (2) Probable cause is unreasonably weak, (3) Warrant is so flawed it's not believable. Example: Judge makes error in warrant - good faith applies. Officer lies in affidavit - no good faith.

  

88. **Independent Source vs. Inevitable Discovery:**

Independent source: Evidence obtained through completely separate lawful means from the illegal conduct. Must be truly independent, not parallel. Inevitable discovery: Evidence would have been discovered anyway through lawful means. Must prove by preponderance that discovery was inevitable. Example: Independent source - two separate investigations. Inevitable discovery - search team would have found evidence anyway.

  

89. **Attenuation Doctrine:**

Considers whether sufficient time/events separate illegal conduct from evidence discovery. Three factors: (1) Temporal proximity between violation and evidence, (2) Intervening circumstances, (3) Flagrancy of the violation. Breaks causal chain between illegal conduct and evidence.

  

90. **Patane Exception:**

Allows admission of physical evidence voluntarily disclosed without full Miranda warnings. Applies when suspect interrupts Miranda and voluntarily provides information. Does NOT apply to coerced confessions - those are never admissible. Example: Suspect says "I know my rights" and voluntarily tells location of gun.

  

91. **Miranda Two-Part Test:**

Requires BOTH custody AND interrogation. Custody factors: Would reasonable person feel free to leave, surroundings, number of officers, tone, duration, restraints. Interrogation: Direct questioning or functional equivalent (words/actions likely to elicit incriminating response).

  

92. **Automobile Exception:**

Justification: Probable cause that vehicle contains evidence/contraband AND vehicle is readily mobile. Scope: May search any part of vehicle that could contain the evidence sought. If PC for entire vehicle = full search. If PC is container-specific = search ends when container found.

  

93. **Terry Frisk:**

Justification: (1) Lawful stop (reasonable suspicion of criminal activity), (2) Reasonable suspicion person is armed and dangerous. Scope: Limited to outer clothing for weapons. Plain feel doctrine: May seize clearly contraband items felt during frisk.

  

94. **Plain View Doctrine:**

Three elements: (1) Officer sees item from location where they have right to be, (2) Immediately recognizes item as evidence ("at first sight" probable cause), (3) Has legal access to seize item. Legal access important because officer must be able to lawfully reach the item to seize it.

  

95. **Curtilage vs. Open Fields:**

Curtilage receives same protection as home. Open fields can be searched with investigative purpose. Four curtilage factors (U.S. v. Dunn): (1) Proximity to home, (2) Included in enclosure, (3) Nature of use, (4) Steps taken to exclude others.

  

96. **Standards of Evidence (Ascending):**

(1) Hunch - no legal value, (2) Reasonably indicative - slight indication, (3) Reasonable suspicion - Terry stop, (4) Probable cause - arrest/search, (5) Preponderance - civil cases, (6) Clear and convincing - highly probable, (7) Beyond reasonable doubt - criminal conviction. Probable cause required for arrest, beyond reasonable doubt for conviction.

  

97. **Search Incident to Arrest:**

Justification: Prevent destruction of evidence, officer safety, prevent escape. Scope varies by location: Public place - full body search and immediate area. Private property - full body search and "Chimel circle" (immediate control area). Vehicle - full body search and interior if arrestee might access vehicle OR reasonable belief vehicle contains evidence.

  

98. **Community Caretaking:**

Very limited function allowing police to check welfare and conduct limited search for identification only. Does NOT authorize full searches. Must be genuine community caretaking purpose, not criminal investigation. Cannot be used as pretext for criminal investigation.

  

99. **Voluntary Consent:**

Must be truly voluntary - no coercion, threats, or promises. Consider tone, number of officers, time, duration. Person must have authority to consent. Can be revoked at any time and limited in scope. Co-occupants can consent unless other present and objects.

  

100. **Exclusionary Rule:**

Purpose: Deter police misconduct, preserve judicial integrity, protect constitutional rights, provide remedy for 4th Amendment violations. Excludes evidence obtained through constitutional violations unless exception applies. Serves as primary enforcement mechanism for constitutional protections.
