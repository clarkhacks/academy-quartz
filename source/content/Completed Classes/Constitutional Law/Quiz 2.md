## PART IV: COMPLEX SCENARIOS (15 Questions)

  

### Multi-Issue Scenarios

  

51. **Complex Scenario:** Officer Johnson stops a vehicle for speeding. The driver appears nervous and admits to having "a small amount of marijuana" in the glove compartment. The officer searches the glove compartment and finds cocaine instead. The driver was never read Miranda rights. Later investigation reveals the driver had outstanding warrants.

  

**Question A:** Is the cocaine admissible in court?

a) No, because Miranda rights were not given

b) Yes, because this was not a custodial interrogation

c) No, because the search exceeded the scope of the traffic stop

d) Yes, because the driver consented to the search

  

**Question B:** Would the analysis change if the driver had been arrested on the warrants first?

a) Yes, Miranda would then be required

b) No, the search would still be valid

c) Yes, the search scope would be different

d) Both a and c

  

52. **Complex Scenario:** Police receive an anonymous tip about drug activity at 123 Main Street. They conduct surveillance and observe suspicious activity. They then illegally enter the home without a warrant and find evidence of drug dealing. Three days later, acting on the original anonymous tip, they obtain a valid warrant and search the same home, finding the same evidence.

  

**Question A:** Is the evidence from the illegal entry admissible?

a) Yes, under good faith exception

b) No, fruit of the poisonous tree applies

c) Yes, under inevitable discovery

d) No, unless attenuation applies

  

**Question B:** Is the evidence from the warranted search admissible?

a) No, fruit of the poisonous tree applies

b) Yes, under the independent source exception

c) No, the good faith exception doesn't apply

d) Yes, under inevitable discovery

  

53. **Complex Scenario:** During a lawful traffic stop, an officer asks the driver to step out of the vehicle. The officer notices a bulge in the driver's jacket and conducts a Terry frisk, finding a gun. The driver had no criminal record and the stop was for a minor traffic violation. During the frisk, the officer also feels what appears to be a small bag of drugs.

  

**Question A:** Was the Terry frisk justified?

a) Yes, officers can always frisk during traffic stops

b) No, there was no reasonable suspicion the person was armed

c) Yes, the bulge provided reasonable suspicion of a weapon

d) No, Terry frisks are not allowed during traffic stops

  

**Question B:** Can the officer seize the suspected drugs?

a) Yes, under plain feel doctrine

b) No, the frisk was only for weapons

c) Yes, if immediately recognizable as contraband

d) Both a and c

  

54. **Complex Scenario:** A suspect is arrested at his home for burglary. During the search incident to arrest, officers search a closed briefcase found in the living room and a locked safe in the bedroom. Inside the briefcase they find evidence of the burglary. The safe contains unrelated stolen property.

  

**Question A:** Is the search of the briefcase valid?

a) Yes, it was within the suspect's immediate control

b) No, closed containers require a separate warrant

c) Yes, under the automobile exception

d) No, the arrest was not valid

  

**Question B:** Is the search of the safe valid?

a) Yes, search incident to arrest covers the entire home

b) No, the safe was not within immediate control

c) Yes, under exigent circumstances

d) No, locked containers always require warrants

  

55. **Complex Scenario:** An officer receives a radio dispatch about a suspect with a gun at a specific address. The dispatch was based on a clerical error - the warrant was actually for a different person. The officer, unaware of the error, arrests the person and finds drugs. Later, it's discovered that the correct suspect was at a different address.

  

**Question A:** Are the drugs admissible?

a) No, the arrest was invalid

b) Yes, under the good faith exception

c) No, the officer should have verified the warrant

d) Yes, under inevitable discovery

  

**Question B:** Would the result change if the officer had reason to doubt the dispatch?

a) Yes, good faith would not apply

b) No, the result would be the same

c) Yes, the officer would need a warrant

d) No, clerical errors are always excused
