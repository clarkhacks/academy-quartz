## Key Constitutional Amendments

### 4th Amendment - Search and Seizure
**Text:** "The right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, and effects, against unreasonable searches and seizures, shall not be violated, and no Warrants shall issue, but upon probable cause..."

**Key Points:**
- Protects against unreasonable searches and seizures
- Requires probable cause for warrants
- Establishes the exclusionary rule foundation
- Does not apply to private citizens (only government agents)

### 5th Amendment - Self-Incrimination and Due Process
**Key Protections:**
- Protection against self-incrimination ("right to remain silent")
- Double jeopardy protection
- Due process clause
- Miranda rights stem from this amendment

### 6th Amendment - Right to Counsel
**Key Rights:**
- Right to a fair and speedy trial
- Right to counsel
- Right to confront witnesses
- Applies during custodial interrogation when invoked

## Standards of Evidence (Ascending Order)

1. **Hunch** - No legal value; just a feeling
2. **Reasonably Indicative** - Slight indication
3. **Reasonable Suspicion** - Can justify stop/detention (Terry Stop)
4. **Probable Cause** - Needed for arrest/search
5. **Preponderance of Evidence** - More likely than not (civil cases)
6. **Clear and Convincing Evidence** - Highly probable
7. **Beyond a Reasonable Doubt** - Required for criminal conviction

## Search & Seizure - Comprehensive Answers

### Terry Frisk
**Legal Requirements:**
1. Must be a **lawful stop** (reasonable suspicion of criminal activity)
2. Must have **reasonable suspicion** that person is armed and dangerous
3. Limited to **outer clothing** for weapons
4. **Plain Feel Doctrine** applies - may seize clearly contraband items

**Factors to Consider:**
- Was suspected crime weapon-related?
- Criminal record of suspect?
- Suspicious behavior?
- Bulge in clothing?
- Attempts to hide something?
- Hands repeatedly moving to pockets?

### Plain View Doctrine
**Three Required Elements:**
1. **Officer must see item from location where they have a right to be**
2. **Must immediately recognize item as evidence** ("at first sight" probable cause)
3. **Must have legal access** to the area to seize the item

**Important:** Plain view is "no search" - officer seizes item without further investigation

### Community Caretaking
**Scope:** Very limited function allowing police to:
- Check on welfare of individuals
- Conduct limited search for identification purposes only
- Does NOT authorize full searches
- Must be genuine community caretaking purpose, not criminal investigation

### Search Warrant Exceptions
**When Warrant Required:** Default rule - get warrant when possible

**Exceptions (ALL/4A):**
- **A**utomobile Exception
- **L**awful Arrest (Search Incident to Arrest)
- **L**awful Consent
- **4A** - Fourth Amendment exceptions:
  - Exigent circumstances
  - Plain view
  - Hot pursuit
  - Emergency aid

## Miranda Rights - Detailed Analysis

### When Miranda Required
**Two-Part Test:** **Custody + Interrogation**

**Custody Factors:**
- Would reasonable person feel free to leave?
- Surroundings and atmosphere
- Number of officers present
- Tone and manner of questioning
- Duration of detention
- Use of restraints

**Interrogation Definition:**
- Direct questioning
- Functional equivalent (words/actions likely to elicit incriminating response)

### Miranda NOT Required
- **Physical evidence** collection
- **Non-coercive, non-custodial** questioning
- **Undercover officers**
- **Private citizens**
- **Statements made outside custody**

### Miranda Invocation Rules
**Must be SPECIFIC and CLEAR:**
- "I want a lawyer" ✓
- "I think I need a lawyer" ✗ (ambiguous)
- "Maybe I should talk to a lawyer" ✗ (ambiguous)

### 14-Day Rule
**Reset Conditions:**
- 14-day break in custody, OR
- New investigator takes over case
- Allows re-approach for Miranda waiver

## Arrest Procedures - Complete Guide

### Probable Cause for Arrest
**Definition:** Facts and circumstances that would lead reasonable person to believe:
- A crime was committed, AND
- This person committed it

**Sources:**
- Personal knowledge/observation
- Reliable informants
- Physical evidence
- Admissions by suspect

### Arrest Authority (RSMo § 544.216)
**Officers may arrest for:**
- Any offense (including traffic violations)
- Infractions with only fine penalties
- Must be POST certified
- Can act on reasonable appearances (even if mistaken)

### Arrest at Residence
**Own Residence:**
- For forcible entry: need reason to believe person inside + (arrest warrant OR exigent circumstances)
- **Cannot arrest for minor crime in private place** (must be felony)
- **DWI = minor crime** per court ruling

**3rd Party Residence:**
- **Felony arrest warrant required**
- **Search warrant for suspect required**
- No consent or emergency = prohibited
- Must protect 3rd-party privacy
- **"Hot pursuit" exception** may allow warrantless entry

## Vehicle Searches - Comprehensive Rules

### Motor Vehicle Exception
**Requirements:**
- **Probable cause** that vehicle contains evidence/contraband
- Vehicle must be readily mobile
- **May search any part** that could contain the evidence sought

**Scope Rules:**
- **If PC for entire vehicle** = full search authorized
- **If PC is container-specific** and container not found = search ends when container located

### Search Incident to Arrest (Vehicle)
**Gant v. Arizona Three-Factor Test:**
1. **Arrestee might access vehicle** to retrieve weapons/evidence, OR
2. **Reasonable belief** that vehicle contains evidence related to arrest, OR
3. **Officer safety concerns**

**Scope:** Interior passenger compartments only (not trunk)

### RV/Mobile Home Rule
- **If detached and affixed to ground** = not mobile, needs warrant
- **If mobile** = treat like vehicle (automobile exception applies)

## Exclusionary Rule - Complete Analysis

### Purpose
**Why it exists:**
- Deter police misconduct
- Preserve judicial integrity
- Protect constitutional rights
- Provide remedy for 4th Amendment violations

### Fruit of the Poisonous Tree
**Doctrine:** Evidence derived from unlawful police conduct is inadmissible
- Primary evidence = poisonous tree
- Secondary evidence = fruit of the tree
- Both are excluded unless exception applies

### Exclusionary Rule Exceptions (Mnemonic: III-GA)

#### 1. **Independent Source**
- Evidence obtained through lawful means completely separate from illegal conduct
- Must be truly independent, not just parallel

#### 2. **Inevitable Discovery**
- Evidence would have been discovered anyway through lawful means
- Must prove by preponderance that discovery was inevitable
- Can involve police or civilian discovery

#### 3. **Intervening Act (Attenuation)**
- Sufficient time/events separate illegal conduct from evidence discovery
- Factors: time elapsed, intervening circumstances, purpose of initial stop
- Example: Illegal stop followed by discovery of valid warrant

#### 4. **Good Faith Exception (U.S. v. Leon)**
- Officer reasonably relied on defective warrant
- Error made by judge, dispatcher, or clerk (not officer)
- Officer's reliance must be objectively reasonable

#### 5. **Attenuation Doctrine**
- Break in causal chain between illegal conduct and evidence
- Considers: temporal proximity, intervening circumstances, flagrancy of violation

### Additional Exceptions

#### **Patane Exception**
- Physical evidence voluntarily submitted without full Miranda warning is admissible
- Example: Suspect interrupts Miranda, says "I know my rights," then voluntarily discloses gun location

#### **Impeachment Exception**
- Uncoerced but inadmissible confessions may be used to challenge defendant's credibility if they testify

## Consent Searches - Detailed Rules

### Voluntary Consent Requirements
**Must be truly voluntary:**
- No coercion, threats, or promises
- Consider: tone of voice, number of officers, time of day, duration
- Person must have authority to consent
- Can be revoked at any time
- Can be limited in scope

### Authority to Consent
**Who can consent:**
- Property owner
- Person with common authority over premises
- Person with apparent authority (if reasonable belief)

**Cannot consent:**
- Guests (to search host's private areas)
- Employees (to employer's private office without authority)
- One co-occupant when other present and objects

## Search Warrants - Complete Procedures

### Missouri Search Warrant Rules
- **Valid for 10 days maximum**
- **Must be executed during daytime (6 AM-10 PM)** unless night service authorized
- **If you leave search area** = new warrant required (unless emergency)
- **Always leave someone on scene**

### Four Steps to Gain Entry
1. **Knock loudly** enough to be heard throughout premises
2. **Announce authority** - "This is [department] police officer"
3. **Announce purpose** - state you have search warrant
4. **Demand entry**

### No-Knock Warrants Allowed When
- Officer believes knocking would create **danger**
- Knocking would create **hostage situation**
- Knocking would result in **evidence destruction**
- Knocking would be **futile**

### Post-Search Requirements
- **Return original warrant** to prosecutor within 2 days
- Leave copy with occupant or in conspicuous place
- Provide receipt for seized items
- **Media invites not allowed** unless necessary

## Special Situations

### Private Party Searches
**Rules:**
- **Private citizens not bound by 4th Amendment**
- **Police can use evidence** found by private parties
- **Officer can repeat search** made by private citizen
- **Cannot go further** than private search
- **Exception:** If private citizen is police agent

### Abandoned Property
**Requirements for Abandonment:**
- **Voluntarily relinquished** interest in property
- **No expectation of privacy**
- **No expectation of returning** to property

**Factors:**
- Left unattended for long period
- Manner of disposal
- Flight from scene
- Denial of ownership
- Condition of property

### Drug Dogs
**Rules:**
- **Dog sniff is not a "search"** (no 4th Amendment protection)
- Must be justified (reasonable suspicion for detention)
- No intrusion into protected area
- Must not unreasonably prolong traffic stop

## Dying Declarations

### Traditional Rule
**Requirements:**
- Declarant must believe death is imminent
- Statement concerns cause/circumstances of impending death
- Declarant must actually die
- Only admissible in homicide prosecutions

### Modern Application
**What if they didn't die?**
- **If declarant available:** Statement is hearsay (generally inadmissible)
- **If declarant unavailable:** May be admissible under other hearsay exceptions
- Consider excited utterance or present sense impression exceptions

## 24-Hour Rule (Missouri Specific)

**Requirement:** If warrantless arrest occurs, warrant application must be filed within 24 hours

**RPAW:** Released Pending Application for Warrant - subject may be released temporarily while waiting for formal warrant

## Problem-Solving Approach

### For Constitutional Law Problems:
1. **Identify the constitutional issue** (4th, 5th, 6th Amendment)
2. **Determine what police action occurred** (search, seizure, interrogation)
3. **Apply appropriate legal standard** (reasonable suspicion, probable cause, etc.)
4. **Consider exceptions** (warrant exceptions, Miranda exceptions, etc.)
5. **Analyze admissibility** (exclusionary rule and exceptions)
6. **Reach conclusion** based on totality of circumstances

### Key Questions to Ask:
- Was there a search or seizure?
- Was it reasonable under the circumstances?
- Did police have proper justification?
- Were proper procedures followed?
- Do any exceptions apply?
- Should evidence be suppressed?

## Search Incident to Arrest - Detailed Analysis

### Scope of Search in Public Place
1. **Full body search** of arrested person
   - Seize weapons (officer safety) and escape tools
   - Seize evidence to prevent destruction
   - Seize contraband (illegal to possess)
   - Includes items immediately associated with suspect's body:
     - Wallet/purse — for weapons/evidence
     - Briefcases, suitcases, etc. — need warrant or consent if locked
     - Property may be impounded while waiting for warrant
2. **Areas around the person** in open fields/public = no expectation of privacy

### Scope of Search on Private Property
1. **Full body search** of arrested person
2. **Search area within immediate control** and just beyond "leaping/lunging" distance
3. **Chimel v. California** — defined the "Chimel Circle"
   - Courts limited search to areas suspect could lunge into
   - Includes containers, drawers, cabinets suspect had access to
   - *"A gun on a table or drawer in front of one arrested is as dangerous as one in the clothing."*
4. **Once suspect is removed**, SIA justification **ends**
   - Can search while transporting
   - Must get new warrant if trying to return later

### Scope of SIA in a Vehicle
1. **Full body search**
2. **Interior search only if:**
   - Occupants aren't secured from vehicle access, **OR**
   - It is **reasonably likely** that vehicle contains evidence of the crime
   - **(Arizona v. Gant, 2009)**
   - **NOTE**: Cannot leave suspect unsecured on purpose to justify SIA
3. **Area within immediate control** or "lunge" range:
   - Same principle as a building
   - Includes glove box and containers (not trunk)
   - **U.S. v. Doward (1994)** — interior includes anything reachable without exiting
   - **U.S. v. Olguin-Rivera (1999)**: Even covered panels reachable from seat = searchable

### Additional SIA Vehicle Rules
- Search includes **area where driver could have thrown evidence**, not just did
- **8th Circuit** agreed with **Barnes (2004)**
- Principle: Areas **reachable without exiting** vehicle may be searched
- Pulling back seat and using flashlight = legal search (Barnes)
- **Closed containers** can be searched
- **Locked containers** may require key, combo, or PC for warrant
- **Searching trunk via drop-down seat access** = legally unclear

### SIA vs. Motor Vehicle Exception (MVE)
**Search Incident to Arrest (SIA):**
- Based on PC to arrest
- No specific knowledge of evidence needed — only *reasonable likelihood*
- Can search person + area to prevent destruction of evidence or weapon access

**Motor Vehicle Exception (MVE):**
- Based on **PC to search**, not arrest
- Must reasonably believe evidence/contraband is in vehicle
- Exception to warrant requirement

## Traffic Stops and Vehicle Searches

### Actions After a Traffic Stop
1. **Order driver or passengers out** — *Maryland v. Wilson (1997)*
2. **Ask for license/insurance** — *Delaware v. Proust (1979)*
3. **Ask questions** — *Berkemeyer v. McCarty (1984)*
   - If not arrested, Miranda not required
4. **Locate and examine VIN** — *U.S. v. Class (1986)*
   - Evidence in plain view during VIN check is admissible
5. **Visual inspection of interior** — *Texas v. Brown (1983)*
   - Only allowed in public/quasi-public locations
6. **Drug dog sniff** — *IL v. Caballes (2005)*
   - No privacy expectation, but must not prolong stop
7. **Control and delay stop only briefly**
8. **Ask for consent to search vehicle**
   - If denied and no R/S or P/C, suspect must be allowed to leave

### Motor Vehicle Exception Searches (MVE)
**For mobile vehicles, MVE = same as search warrant in justification/scope**

#### A. Justification
- PC that evidence is in vehicle
- Must be apparently mobile
- PC to arrest ≠ PC to search

#### B. Scope
- Every part of vehicle and contents
- Must relate to **object of search** (evidence, contraband, weapons)
- Can frisk if separate R/S that occupant is armed

#### C. Containers
1. **Any container** may be searched (open, closed, locked) if PC exists
2. **Container-specific PC**: Search ends if item not found
   - Can't expand scope unless PC shifts
3. **Ownership doesn't matter** — unassociated passenger items may be searched

#### D. Time/Location
1. Search can be **immediate**
2. Can be **delayed** if PC was developed before vehicle seized
3. Can search on **private property** with lawful access
4. **Mobile homes = cars** if mobile — *CA v. Carney*
   - Trailers need to meet "mobile" criteria

**NOTE**: Must establish PC **before vehicle is made immobile**

## Emergency and Exigent Circumstances

### Searches Under Emergency/Exigent Circumstances
**Justification:**
- PC to believe emergency exists (e.g., screaming, fire, destruction risk)
- Ends when emergency ends — then need new justification

**Scope:**
- Limited to **where emergency requires**
- May seize items in **plain view**

### Curtilage as Crime Scene
- **Private property does not become public** because a crime happened
- Must still have **lawful interest** to enter

## Hearsay Rule Exceptions

### A. Admissions and Confessions
- Reliable if obtained voluntarily and with Constitutional Rights advisement
- See Exclusionary Rule

### B. Dying Declarations
- Declarant must believe they are dying and be permanently unable to testify
- Applies even if not technically dead (coma, unresponsive, etc.)
- **If person recovers, declaration invalid**

### C. Spontaneous Statements
- Made without time to fabricate (e.g., stressful events)
- Can be corroborated after occurrence

### D. Present Memory Refreshed
- Officer refreshes memory from report but must testify from memory
- Opposing party may inspect report and cross-examine

### E. Past Recollection Recorded
- When officer can't remember even with report
- Officer must verify report was made when memory was fresh
- **Writing itself becomes the evidence**

### F. Prior Testimony
- Testimony from earlier court proceedings may be allowed if person unavailable

### G. Unidentified Sources
**Informants with protected identity may be admissible if:**
1. Proven track record of reliability
2. Testifies against self/others
3. Provided detailed, compelling info
4. Info corroborated by officer

### H. Public Records
- Admissible if no reason to suspect falsification
- Assumed accurate due to lack of motive for falsifying
- Includes minutes, tax records, license applications
- **Not valid for lab tech reports**

### I. Business Records
- Assumed accurate and usable if person who made them is absent
- Not admissible if recordkeeping process itself is in question
- Examples: sales receipts, contracts, bills of lading

## Contact, Detention, and Arrest - Expanded

### Three Levels of Police Contact

| Level | Justification | Force | Search | Miranda |
|-------|---------------|-------|--------|---------|
| **Voluntary Contact** | Mere suspicion | None | None (except caretaking) | Not required |
| **Detention** | Reasonable suspicion | Reasonable, non-deadly | Frisk of outer clothing for weapons | Not required unless clearly arrest |
| **Arrest** | Probable cause | Reasonable, possibly deadly | Full body | Required |

**Key**: A reasonable person feels free to leave = voluntary contact
**Note**: Unreasonable use of **time, force, or movement** = **de facto arrest**

### Reasonable Suspicion vs Probable Cause - Detailed

**Reasonable Suspicion** – Facts leading reasonable person to *suspect*:
1. A crime occurred/is occurring/is about to occur
2. The person was criminally involved

**Probable Cause** – Facts leading reasonable person to *believe*:
1. A crime occurred/is occurring
2. The person to be arrested was criminally involved

### Sources of Facts for RS and PC
- **Officer Knowledge**: Evidence, time of day, suspect reputation, demeanor, etc.
- **Flight**: Unexplained flight is a factor; running from restricted area = RS
- **Info from Reliable Persons**: LEOs, federal agents, dispatchers, known good citizens
- **Criminal Informants** (see Unidentified Sources above)
- **Anonymous Tips**: Usually not RS unless predictive + corroborated
  - MO SC: non-predictive tips ≠ RS
- **Note**: *Totality of circumstances* applies (*IL v. Wardlow*, 2000)

## Curtilage and Open Fields - Comprehensive

### Essential Terms
1. **Private Property** – Requires warrant or valid exception
2. **Curtilage** – Requires same justification as premises
3. **Open Field** – Can be searched if LEO shows investigative purpose
4. **Public Area** – No legitimate expectation of privacy
5. **Quasi-Public Area** – Limited privacy expectation

### Four Curtilage Factors (*U.S. v. Dunn* – 1987)
1. **Proximity to the home**
2. **Included in enclosure**
3. **Nature of use**
4. **Steps taken to exclude others**

### Curtilage Applications
- In suburbs, curtilage = yard; in rural, depends on interpretation
- **Quasi-public areas**: Walks from street to front door, front porches, pathways with no access restriction
- **No business curtilage**
- Public/private areas of buildings vary in privacy expectation
- **Surveillance allowed** from public or quasi-public areas
- Non-curtilage private property has reduced privacy
- Officers may treat fenced land as **open field** if they have **investigative purpose**
- Fenced property ≠ 4th Amendment violation if LEO has investigative purpose

**Note**: *Reasonableness* is the key standard

### Evidence/contraband can be seized without warrant if:

#### For Surveillance
- Officer sees item from **lawful vantage point**

#### For Seizure
- Officer has **probable cause** that item is evidence/contraband
- Officer has **legal access** to area (**threshold rule**)

## Private Party Searches - Detailed

### Rules
- **4th Amendment doesn't apply** to private individuals
- Evidence from private searches ≠ 4th Amendment issue
- **LEO can repeat private search** if:
  - Officer didn't direct it
  - Officer doesn't intrude further than private person
  - Info used to justify warrant or exigent entry

## Exclusionary Rule Case Law

### Major Cases
- **Weeks v. U.S.** (1914)
- **Mapp v. Ohio** (1961)
- **Silverthorne Lumber Co. v. U.S.** (1920)

### Fruit of the Poisonous Tree
- **Tainted evidence = inadmissible**
- Applies to evidence **derived** from illegal source
- **Exception**: *U.S. v. Patane* (2004) – voluntary confession w/o Miranda = admissible if physical evidence seized properly
- **Coerced confessions = never admissible**

### Good Faith Exception - Detailed
**Major Cases**: *U.S. v. Leon* (1984), *Mass. v. Sheppard* (1984), *AR v. Evans* (1995)

**Allowed when**:
- Officer reasonably believes warrant is valid
- Judicial or clerical error

**NOT Allowed when**:
1. Officer falsifies or fails to verify info
2. PC is unreasonably weak
3. Warrant is so flawed it's not believable

**Note**: Fruit of the Poisonous Tree still applies unless **exception** applies
